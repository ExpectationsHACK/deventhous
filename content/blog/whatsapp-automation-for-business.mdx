For most businesses in Nigeria, WhatsApp is where sales happen. Customers ask about prices, check availability, and place orders there. The problem is that people expect a reply quickly, and nobody can watch their phone all day and all night.

That's what WhatsApp automation is for. Here is what it can realistically do, and how to start.

## What automation can handle

### Instant replies to common questions

Most businesses answer the same questions every day: prices, opening hours, delivery areas, and whether something is in stock. An AI assistant trained on your business can answer these in seconds, at any hour, in a natural way.

### Lead follow-up

Someone fills in your website form or sends a message and then goes quiet. Automation can follow up a few hours later, and again a day or two after that, so good leads don't slip away just because everyone was busy.

### Bookings and reminders

For clinics, salons, consultants, and anyone who works by appointment, automation can offer open slots, confirm bookings, and send reminders the day before. Reminders are one of the simplest ways to reduce no-shows.

### Missed-call text-back

When you miss a call, an automatic WhatsApp message goes out: "Sorry we missed you, how can we help?" The customer gets a reply straight away instead of calling your competitor.

### Review requests

After a job is done, a short message asks happy customers to leave a Google review. More reviews help you show up in local search.

## What it should not do

Automation should not pretend to be a person, and it should not trap customers in a loop. A good setup:

- Says clearly that it's an assistant
- Hands off to a real person when asked, or when a question is outside what it knows
- Sends you a summary so you can pick up the conversation where it left off

## What you need to get started

1. **A WhatsApp Business account.** For automated replies at scale, you'll need the WhatsApp Business API through Meta. Meta charges its own messaging fees.
2. **Your business information.** Prices, services, hours, delivery areas, policies, and answers to your most common questions.
3. **A clear handoff plan.** Who gets notified when a customer needs a person, and how.

## Where to start

Pick the one problem that costs you the most. For a shop, it's usually slow replies to price questions. For an appointment business, it's usually no-shows. Automate that first, check it works, then add the next one.
