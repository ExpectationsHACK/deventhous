Ask five web designers in Lagos what a website costs and you will get five very different answers. That isn't because anyone is lying. It's because "a website" can mean anything from a single page to a full online store with payments, courses, and automation.

This guide explains what actually drives the price, so you can compare quotes properly and avoid paying for things you don't need, or missing things you do.

## 1. What the site has to do

This is the biggest factor. Start with the job, not the number of pages.

- **Get enquiries.** A focused landing page or a small site with a contact form and WhatsApp button.
- **Sell products.** A catalogue, product pages, a cart or WhatsApp ordering, and payment integration.
- **Publish content.** A blog and a content admin so your team can post without a developer.
- **Teach.** A course platform with student accounts and progress tracking.
- **Work after hours.** AI assistants, automatic follow-ups, and booking reminders.

Each step up adds design, development, and testing time. That's where most of the price difference comes from.

## 2. Payments

Accepting payments with Paystack or Flutterwave means more than adding a button. Someone has to handle failed payments, order confirmation emails, receipts, and refunds. If a quote includes "payment integration" for very little, ask exactly what happens when a payment fails.

## 3. Content and copy

Who writes the words and supplies the photos? Many projects stall because content isn't ready. Some agencies include copywriting; others expect you to provide everything. Neither is wrong, but the price should reflect it.

## 4. SEO

Every decent site should ship with the basics: page titles, descriptions, a sitemap, and fast load times. That's not the same as an SEO project, which adds keyword research, structured data, local SEO, and a content plan. If you need customers to find you on Google, budget for real SEO work. If you mostly send traffic from Instagram or ads, the basics may be enough for now.

## 5. Hosting, domain, and support

The build is a one-time cost. Running the site is not. Ask about:

- **Domain**: renewed every year, and it should be registered in your name
- **Hosting**: where the site lives, and who pays for it
- **Support**: who fixes things when they break, and who makes small changes

A cheap build with no support can cost more over a year than a fair build with support included.

## Questions to ask before you pay

1. Will I own the site, the code, and the domain once I've paid?
2. What exactly is included, and what costs extra?
3. How many rounds of design changes are included?
4. How long will it take, and what do you need from me?
5. What happens after launch?
